## Equipment Needed

- 4n + 1 (where n = 1, 2, 3...) of any objects. For example, 21 rubber ducks (from Penguins, Poo and Populations, in Maths A) or 13 wooden pegs (in Floating) on a clipboard. 

<br/>

## Experiment Explanation

### Rules
This is a two-player game. Players take turns to take ducks from the pool. A player can take up to three ducks on their turn. The player who takes the final duck loses. 

### Strategy
The winning strategy is to go second, and, on your turn, take 4 minus the number of ducks your opponent has taken (i.e. if they take 1 duck, you should take 3). 

### How to Demonstrate
Play a few rounds with the child(ren) until they are suitably frustrated and want to learn the strategy. To help them work it out, you can put the ducks into groups of four. Go second, and make it increasingly obvious that you are emptying each group of four on your turn.
