# Scale of the Solar System

## Equipment Needed 
- Golf ball - Moon
- Globe - Earth
- Marbles - Earth/Sun on different scales
- Red foam ball - Jupiter
- Card with pin-prick hole - Sun/Solar system on bigger scales
- Diagram/pictures of solar system

## Experiment Explanation 
Place the different objects at varying distances and combinations to give kids a sense of scale of how big objects in the Solar System are, and how far they are from each other.

The kid may not necessarily know all of the planets in the Solar System yet, so you can ask them to name the ones they know and fill in the rest. Feel free to throw in some facts about them to get them interested.

Ask the kids if they know what the globe is - most of them are able to say that it's a model of our planet. Point out where we are and stress how small the country is compared to he size of the entire planet. At this point, present them with a few different sized balls from the box: a golf ball is the right size to represent the Moon at the scale of the globe, but ask them what they think. I usually use a marble, one of the small polystyrene balls, a golf ball, and the foam football. Once you reveal that the golf ball is the correct ball, I like to drop in that the Moon is about the same width as Australia and hold the ball up to the globe to prove it. Then ask them to take a golf ball and place it how far they think the Moon is from the Earth at this scale (which should be **4 metres between the globe Earth and golf ball Moon**). At this scale it is worth noting that **the speed of light is roughly 3m/s**, since the Moon is ~1.3 light seconds away.

Now zoom out a bit, so the Earth has shrunk down from the size of the globe to the size of a marble. How big is the Sun on this scale? The kids will probably point to the foam football because you've primed them to think about the balls in the box. This is good logic, but quite far off - the **football is the size of Jupiter** at this scale, and **the Sun is 2 metres across**, higher than (probably) but still on the order of your height. At this scale, **the speed of light is roughly 0.4m/s, Earth is 200 metres away, and Jupiter is over a kilometre away**.

Zoom out even more! Let's make the Sun the size of a marble. At this scale the Earth would be practically invisible (about 0.1mm across) so we will relax the scale requirements on the size of the planets and just focus on the distances between them, using marbles to represent their locations. Showing all of the planets may take a while, so I'd stress just doing **Earth, Jupiter, and Neptune at 2m, 10m, 60m from the Sun respectively**. At this scale we can start to think about how far away other stars are. The nearest system to our own is the Alpha Centauri system - a set of three stars (Alpha Centauri A and B, orbiting each other very closely, and Proxima Centauri, which orbits the other two at a much further out radius). At this scale, the **Alpha Centauri system is still 543km away from us** (roughly the distance from London ton Edinburgh as the crow flies) and **the speed of light is roughly 4mm/s**.

Finally we zoom out even more so that the Sun is the size of a pinprick hole in a piece of card. **Neptune would be 4m away, and Alpha Centauri is still 29km away**. Use this to really emphasise the enormous distances between stars in the solar neighbourhood compared to the distances within our own solar system and the sizes of objects like stars and planets. *For older audiences, they might already be familiar with the concept of a gas and that gas particles in air are constantly colliding with each other - this is because the distances between air molecules and the sizes of air molecules are much more comparable than the distances between stars and star sizes. This could lead in to talking about galaxy mergers: when two galaxies merge, the odds of even a single collision between stars is abolutely negligible, instead what happens is large gas clouds (nebulae) collide with each other, and stars flow around gravitationally without colliding.* 

Each of the different scales and corresponding distances are listed here for ease:

Globe Earth:
- Golf Ball Moon at a distance of 4m
- Speed of light c = 3m/s

Marble Earth:
- 2m diameter of the Sun
- Earth 200m from the Sun
- foam football Jupiter, > 1km from the Sun
- Speed of light 0.4m/s

Marble Sun:
- Mercury 0.8m
- Venus 1.5m
- Earth 2m
- Mars 3m
- Jupiter 10m
- Saturn 19m
- Uranus 38m
- Neptune 60m
- Alpha Centauri 543km (London - Edinburgh)
- Speed of light 4mm/s

Pinprick hole Sun:
- Neptune 4m
- Alpha Centauri 29km

## Risk Assessment

### **Hazard**: Balls/marbles

**Description**: Tripping or falling over stray rolling balls/marbles.

**Affected People**: All

**Before Mitigation**: Likelihood: 4, Severity: 3, Overall: 12 (High)

**Mitigation**: Demonstrator must emphasise that children must walk, should stop everyone walking to actually do explanations. Take note of any trip hazards (cables, other experiments, etc.) that may block the path of children walking & warn them to be careful of these if necessary.
Must be made very clear to children that they may not kick or throw the balls ever.
Call first aider in case of injury.

**After Mitigation**: Likelihood: 2, Severity: 3, Overall: 6 (Medium)

<br/>

### **Hazard**: Small parts (marbles)

**Description**: Small balls used in some parts of the experiment being swallowed with potential of choking.

**Affected People**: Children

**Before Mitigation**: Likelihood: 2, Severity: 5, Overall: 10 (High)

**Mitigation**: Do not use small balls parts of experiment for audiences of very young children.
Keep track of all small parts.  Don't lose your marbles!
Call a first aider in the event of swallowing/choking

**After Mitigation**: Likelihood: 1, Severity: 5, Overall: 5 (Medium)
